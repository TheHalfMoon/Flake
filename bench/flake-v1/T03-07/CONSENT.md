# T03-07 — consent and privacy package (v1)

This is the exact material given to a candidate participant before any eligibility screening question that reveals study content, and before any timed activity begins. It is written in plain language, not legal boilerplate, because a participant must actually understand it, not merely sign it.

## 1. What this study is

You are being asked to help compare two ways of picking a personal or work project back up after a break: one using a tool called Flake, and one using a plainly maintained set of Markdown files. You will do this eight times, each time with different (fictional) project material, so we are not asking about your own real project's private content unless you choose to bring some of your own non-sensitive material (section 6).

## 2. What you will actually do

- A short training walkthrough (about 20 minutes) showing you both ways of working, using a practice example that is not part of the real comparison.
- Eight rounds ("pairs"). In each pair you will set up a small project twice — once in Flake, once in the Markdown workflow — using different but similarly-sized material each time. After setting one up, you will do a short unrelated 5-minute activity (to simulate stepping away), then come back and answer a question about the project as if you were resuming your own work.
- Each setup step has a fixed 10-minute limit, and each "coming back" step has a fixed 8-minute limit, the same for both ways of working.
- The order in which you use Flake versus the Markdown workflow is decided in advance by a fixed schedule, not by us during your session.

## 3. Time commitment

Sixteen attempts total (eight pairs), each attempt bounded to 10 (setup) + 5 (break) + 8 (resume) minutes plus a short break between pairs, roughly 4-6 hours in total, which may be split across more than one session within the same week if that is easier for you.

## 4. Participation is optional

You do not have to take part, and you may stop at any point, for any reason, without needing to explain why and without any negative consequence. If you stop partway through a round that round is simply recorded as incomplete; anything you already finished stays recorded unless you specifically ask us to delete it (section 8).

## 5. What we record

- Timings for each phase of each attempt (how long setup took, how long the break was, how long resuming took).
- Whether you answered the resume question correctly, incorrectly, or ran out of time.
- Which condition (Flake or Markdown) and which round each attempt belongs to.
- Notes about anything that went wrong with the study process itself (e.g., a tool crashed).

What we do not record: the actual text you type into either tool, your keystrokes, screen recordings, audio, or video, unless you explicitly opt in to a specific redacted excerpt being quoted (section 6).

## 6. Your own material and data minimization

You are never required to use your own real project or any sensitive material. All eight rounds use prepared fictional material identical in structure and difficulty for everyone. If you choose to bring your own non-sensitive material instead for any round, tell the facilitator beforehand; nothing you type is captured into the study record unless you separately and explicitly agree, in writing, to a specific excerpt being quoted, and even then it is reviewed for anything identifying before it is used.

## 7. Identification, storage, and retention

You will be referred to only by a study code (like "P3") in every recorded file, including files that may become part of a public, open-source repository. Nothing that identifies you by name, email, or other direct identifier is written into that repository. If a mapping from your study code to your real identity needs to exist at all (for example, to schedule your sessions), it is kept separately from the repository, is not shared beyond the facilitator, and follows a fixed retention limit: deletion no later than 90 days after the study's results are finalized.

## 8. Withdrawal and deletion

You can withdraw at any time. By default, any attempts you already completed remain part of the study record, because they already happened under the same rules as everyone else's. If you would prefer your already-completed attempts be deleted entirely instead of kept, tell the facilitator and this will be honored and recorded as a withdrawal-with-deletion.

## 9. What becomes public

Timing numbers, outcome codes (correct/incorrect/timed out), and which round/condition each belongs to may be published in a public repository as part of this project's open evidence record, always under your study code only. No file contents you typed, no names, and no other identifying detail are published by default.

## 10. Who to contact

If you have questions before, during, or after the study, ask the facilitator running your session. There is no requirement to continue once you have started, and asking a question is never treated as a reason to end your participation early unless you choose to end it.

## 11. Informed consent statement

By proceeding, you give informed consent, confirming that:

- you have read this document in full,
- you understand what will be recorded and what will not,
- you understand participation is voluntary and you may withdraw at any time,
- you agree to take part on this basis.

Recorded consent (written or typed, dated, tied only to your study code) is obtained before any timed activity begins, and re-confirmed at the start of a second session if your two sessions fall on different days.
